# Underlying Theory of Microlocal Analysis and Semiclassical Approximations

[Back to psipy main page](./psipy.md)

This document presents the mathematical foundations of the `microlocal` toolkit, as described in the accompanying code and documentation. The theory is drawn from classical references such as Hörmander [1], Duistermaat [2], Maslov & Fedoriuk [3], Zworski [4], and Taylor [5].

## 1. Principal Symbol and the Characteristic Variety

Let $P$ be a linear partial differential operator (or more generally a pseudodifferential operator) on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Its **principal symbol** $p(x,\xi)$ is a smooth function on the cotangent bundle $T^*\mathbb{R}^n \cong \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n$, homogeneous in $\xi$ for classical operators. For a differential operator
$$P = \sum_{|\alpha|\le m} a_\alpha(x) D^\alpha, \qquad D = -i\partial,$$
the principal symbol is
$$p(x,\xi) = \sum_{|\alpha|=m} a_\alpha(x) \xi^\alpha.$$

The **characteristic variety** (or characteristic set) of $P$ is the zero set of the principal symbol:
$$\operatorname{Char}(P) = \{ (x,\xi) \in T^*\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\} \mid p(x,\xi)=0 \}.$$
It encodes the phase‑space directions in which $P$ fails to be elliptic. Singularities of solutions to $Pu = f$ (with smooth $f$) can only propagate inside $\operatorname{Char}(P)$.

## 2. Bicharacteristic Flow

The Hamiltonian vector field of the symbol $p$ is
$$X_p = \frac{\partial p}{\partial \xi}\cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial \xi}.$$
Its integral curves $(x(t),\xi(t))$ satisfy **Hamilton’s equations**:
$$\dot{x} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial \xi},\qquad \dot{\xi} = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}.$$
These curves are called **bicharacteristics**. They lie inside the characteristic variety when the initial condition satisfies $p(x_0,\xi_0)=0$, because $p$ is constant along the flow:
$$\frac{d}{dt}p(x(t),\xi(t)) = \{p,p\} = 0.$$

For a $2$‑dimensional spatial domain ($n=2$), the variables are $(x,y,\xi,\eta)$ and the equations become
$$\dot{x} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial\xi},\quad \dot{y} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial\eta},\quad
\dot{\xi} = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial x},\quad \dot{\eta} = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}.$$

## 3. Wavefront Set

The **wavefront set** $\operatorname{WF}(u)$ of a distribution $u$ is a closed conical subset of $T^*\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\{0\}$ that refines the singular support: it tells not only where $u$ is singular, but also the directions (frequencies) of the singularity. A point $(x_0,\xi_0)$ is **not** in $\operatorname{WF}(u)$ if there exists a smooth cutoff $\phi$ with $\phi(x_0)=1$ and a conic neighbourhood $\Gamma$ of $\xi_0$ such that the Fourier transform $\widehat{\phi u}(\xi)$ decays rapidly as $|\xi|\to\infty$ for $\xi\in\Gamma$.

The fundamental theorem of microlocal analysis states:
- $\operatorname{WF}(Pu) \subseteq \operatorname{WF}(u)$ (pseudolocal property).
- $\operatorname{WF}(u) \setminus \operatorname{WF}(Pu) \subseteq \operatorname{Char}(P)$.
- The complement $\operatorname{WF}(u) \setminus \operatorname{WF}(Pu)$ is invariant under the bicharacteristic flow of $p$.

Thus singularities propagate along bicharacteristics inside the characteristic variety.

## 4. WKB (Semiclassical) Approximation

For a small parameter $\varepsilon>0$ (often identified with $\hbar$), we seek approximate solutions of $P_\varepsilon u_\varepsilon = 0$ in the form
$$u_\varepsilon(x) \sim e^{iS(x)/\varepsilon} \sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k(x) \varepsilon^k.$$
Inserting this ansatz into $P_\varepsilon u_\varepsilon = 0$ and equating powers of $\varepsilon$ yields:

- **Eikonal equation** (order $\varepsilon^{-m}$):
  $$p(x,\nabla S(x)) = 0.$$
  The phase $S$ is a solution of the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, and its level sets are the wavefronts.

- **Transport equations** (order $\varepsilon^{-m+k}$):
  $$ \mathcal{L}_p a_k + \text{(lower order terms)} = 0,$$
  where $\mathcal{L}_p$ is the derivative along the bicharacteristic flow generated by $p$. The leading amplitude $a_0$ satisfies the homogeneous transport equation, and higher corrections are obtained recursively.

In practice, the WKB solution is constructed by tracing rays (bicharacteristics) from an initial surface and integrating the amplitude along them.

## 5. Caustics and the Maslov Index

The WKB approximation breaks down at points where rays focus – **caustics**. At a caustic, the Jacobian of the ray map vanishes, and the amplitude becomes infinite. Uniform asymptotic expansions near caustics involve special functions (Airy for fold caustics, Pearcey for cusps, etc.) that replace the divergent WKB amplitude.

The **Maslov index** $\mu$ is an integer that counts (with sign) the number of caustics encountered along a ray. It appears as a phase shift $e^{i\mu\pi/2}$ in the semiclassical quantization condition and in the uniform approximation. For a closed orbit in one dimension, the Maslov index is typically $2$ (two turning points), giving the familiar factor $e^{i\pi/2}$ for each turning point.

## 6. Bohr–Sommerfeld Quantisation (1D)

For a one‑dimensional bound state problem with Hamiltonian $H(x,p) = \frac{p^2}{2} + V(x)$, the semiclassical quantisation condition is
$$\frac{1}{2\pi\hbar} \oint p(x,E)\,dx = n + \frac{\mu}{4}, \qquad n=0,1,2,\dots,$$
where the integral is over one period of the classical motion, $p(x,E) = \sqrt{2(E-V(x))}$, and $\mu$ is the Maslov index (usually $2$ for a potential well with two turning points, so $\mu/4 = 1/2$). This is the **Bohr–Sommerfeld rule**.

The action variable is
$$I(E) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \oint p(x,E)\,dx,$$
and the quantised energies are determined by $I(E_n) = \hbar\,(n+\mu/4)$.

## 7. Stability Matrix and Caustic Detection

For a family of rays starting from an initial surface, the **stability matrix** $J(t)$ (also called the Jacobian of the ray map) is defined as
$$J(t) = \frac{\partial (x(t),y(t))}{\partial (x_0,y_0)}.$$
It satisfies the linearised Hamiltonian system
$$\frac{dJ}{dt} = H_{px}\,J, \qquad J(0)=I_2,$$
where $H_{px}$ is the matrix of mixed derivatives
$$H_{px} = \begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{\partial^2 p}{\partial\xi\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial^2 p}{\partial\xi\partial y}\\[6pt]
\dfrac{\partial^2 p}{\partial\eta\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial^2 p}{\partial\eta\partial y}
\end{pmatrix}.$$
A caustic occurs when $\det J(t)=0$ (focusing of rays). The Maslov index along a ray is the number of sign changes of $\det J(t)$ (or more rigorously, the intersection number of the ray with the caustic set in the Lagrangian manifold).

## 8. Catastrophe Classification (Arnold’s Theory)

The local geometry of caustics is classified by the **Arnold (or catastrophe) types**. For a family of rays depending on parameters, the phase function exhibits critical points whose degeneracy determines the caustic. The simplest types are:

- **Fold ($A_2$)** – generic caustic; the amplitude involves the Airy function $\operatorname{Ai}$.
- **Cusp ($A_3$)** – the Pearcey function appears.
- **Swallowtail ($A_4$)** and higher are also possible.

The classification is based on the corank of the Hessian of the phase and the number of parameters.

## 9. Summary of Key Objects

| Object | Symbol / Definition | Role |
|--------|---------------------|------|
| Principal symbol | $p(x,\xi)$ | Determines characteristic variety and flow |
| Characteristic variety | $\{p=0\}$ | Support of singularities |
| Bicharacteristics | Hamilton’s equations | Propagation curves |
| Wavefront set | $\operatorname{WF}(u)$ | Phase‑space singular support |
| Eikonal equation | $p(x,\nabla S)=0$ | Phase of WKB solution |
| Transport equation | $\mathcal{L}_p a_0=0$ | Amplitude along rays |
| Maslov index | $\mu$ | Phase correction at caustics |
| Stability matrix | $J = \partial x/\partial x_0$ | Detects caustics ($\det J=0$) |
| Bohr–Sommerfeld | $\frac{1}{2\pi\hbar}\oint p\,dx = n+\frac{\mu}{4}$ | Semiclassical quantisation |
